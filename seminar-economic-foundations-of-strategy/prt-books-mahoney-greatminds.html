两本书中的产权理论：Mahoney (2005) 与 Great Minds (2005)

来源：① Joseph T. Mahoney,《Economic Foundations of Strategy》(Sage, 2005) 第 3 章「Property Rights Theory」；② Ken G. Smith & Michael A. Hitt (eds.),《Great Minds in Management: The Process of Theory Development》(Oxford, 2005) 第 23 章 Oliver E. Williamson「Transaction Cost Economics」中对产权理论的讨论。

一句话总结

Mahoney 的书把产权理论（property rights theory, PRT）作为一个独立章节，梳理了从 Coase (1960) 到 Alchian–Demsetz、再到 Grossman–Hart–Moore 的完整脉络；Great Minds 那本书则没有产权理论的专章，产权理论是作为交易成本经济学（TCE）的「姊妹理论」在 Williamson 自述理论发展时被反复提及——Coase 定理、古典产权文献、以及 A&D 与 GHM 对 TCE 的两处关键交锋。

一、Mahoney (2005)《Economic Foundations of Strategy》第 3 章：产权理论

Mahoney 把组织经济学（organizational economics）分为五个理论，产权理论位列其中（行为理论、交易成本理论、产权理论、代理理论、资源基础/动态能力）。第 3 章回答的基本问题是：企业如何创造与配置财富？

1.1 古典产权理论：乐观的效率演化论

  • 起点是 Coase (1960)，高潮是 Alchian & Demsetz (1973)。
  • Demsetz (1967) 是新古典经济学传统的典范，乐观地认为产权会向经济效率演化。
  • 产权效率的三个标准：普遍性（universality，所有稀缺资源都有主）、排他性（exclusivity，权利是排他的）、可转让性（transferability，资源能从低收益用途流向高收益用途）。

1.2 对乐观论的批判：效率演化常常失败

  • Libecap (1989) 与 North (1990) 用历史研究挑战「产权必然向效率演化」的乐观判断。
  • 核心机制：分配冲突（distributional conflicts）给政治家带来政治风险，于是他们会提出「不严重动摇现状排名」的规制，只对公共池塘资源（common-pool）损失提供有限救济；监管机构也有同样的既得利益。
  • 历史重要（history matters）：今天的选择受昨天决策的约束。North 尤其关注那些导致经济停滞与衰退的制度框架，而不只是成功。

1.3 其他古典脉络

  • Eggertsson (1990) 三层分析：① 产权结构被显式建模但外生；② 组织形式内生、但产权基本结构外生；③ 把社会/政治规则与政治制度结构都视为内生。
  • Barzel (1989)：因为精确测量资产全部属性成本高昂，权利永远无法被完全界定，财产因而面临被他人侵占的危险（逆向选择、搭便车、偷懒）。

1.4 现代产权理论：Grossman–Hart–Moore

  • Hart (1990, 1995) 把「契约不完全性（contractual incompleteness）」与「控制（control）」两个概念结合，回答四个问题：(a) 所有权意味着什么？(b) 什么决定企业边界？(c) 契约不完全性的经济含义？(d) 非人力资产与权威的角色？
  • 所有权 = 剩余控制权（residual control rights）。买 vs 租：零交易成本下二者等价；在正交易成本 + 契约不完全的世界里，所有者掌握契约未写明的用途的决定权，因此有更强的激励去维护机器、学会正确操作、并购置能与之形成协同的其他机器。
  • 企业边界：边界的选择是为了在各方之间最优配置控制权。高度互补的资产若分属不同所有者，谁都无法真正控制对方，合并（把全部控制权给一方）能增进价值。
  • 与其他理论的关系：委托—代理理论假设契约可以零成本地写完备，因此控制权无关紧要；TCE 与 PRT 最接近，但 TCE 更强调写契约的成本与由此导致的契约不完全性，较少关注「制度安排如何配置控制权」。

1.5 前瞻

Mahoney 判断：随着信息时代知识产权（intellectual property rights）日益重要，产权理论在战略管理中将得到更多关注，是未来研究的增长领域。

二、Great Minds (2005)：Williamson 第 23 章（TCE）里的产权理论

Great Minds 没有产权理论的专章；产权理论出现在 Williamson 自述交易成本经济学发展过程的第 23 章里，作为 TCE 的姊妹理论出现四处关键讨论：

2.1 Coase 定理与产权配置

在零交易成本下，谈判会达成财富最大化的安排，与初始的产权配置无关；一旦引入正交易成本，产权配置就变得重要。（Coase 1960，转引自 Williamson，p. 491）

2.2 古典产权文献滋养了 TCE

Williamson 回忆他在宾大（Penn）组织的两学期组织理论课，广泛吸收了他 Carnegie 的背景、社会学、「产权经济学文献」、经理自由裁量文献、以及市场失灵文献。

2.3 A&D (1972) 对「权威/fiat」的反驳

「那篇著名的 Alchian & Demsetz (1972) 论文……对企业与市场之间的『命令（fiat）』差异不以为然。」（脚注 15，p. 498）

这是 A&D 与 Williamson 的直接交锋：Williamson 认为企业拥有市场所没有的「命令与权威」（fiat、协调适应的治理结构）；A&D 则认为企业与市场没有权威上的差别，区别只在于「团队生产 + 中央缔约方」。

2.4 GHM (1986) 抽象掉了治理差异

「『企业的产权理论』（Grossman & Hart, 1986）把这些（fiat、治理结构等）差异全部假设掉了。」（脚注 16，p. 499）

Williamson 的立场：现代产权理论（GHM）虽与 TCE 同源，但它在模型里抽象掉了 TCE 强调的治理结构差异，聚焦于事前投资激励与剩余控制权。

三、两本书合起来看

  • Mahoney (2005) 给的是产权理论的「全景教科书式梳理」：古典（Coase/Demsetz/A&D/Barzel/Eggertsson）→ 批判（Libecap/North 的「效率演化会失败」）→ 现代（Hart/GHM 的剩余控制权与企业边界）。
  • Great Minds (2005) 给的是产权理论在 TCE 发展史中的「关系定位」：Coase 定理是共同起点，古典产权文献滋养了 TCE，而 A&D 与 GHM 分别从「无权威」与「抽象掉治理」两个方向对 TCE 提出挑战或互补。
  • 两者互为补充：Mahoney 讲「产权理论本身是什么」，Great Minds 讲「产权理论在组织经济学版图中与 TCE 如何对话」。

Property Rights Theory in Two Books: Mahoney (2005) & Great Minds (2005)

Sources: ① Joseph T. Mahoney, Economic Foundations of Strategy (Sage, 2005), Ch. 3 "Property Rights Theory"; ② Ken G. Smith & Michael A. Hitt (eds.), Great Minds in Management: The Process of Theory Development (Oxford, 2005), Ch. 23, Oliver E. Williamson, "Transaction Cost Economics", where property rights theory is discussed.

One-sentence summary

Mahoney's book gives property rights theory (PRT) its own chapter, tracing the full line from Coase (1960) through Alchian–Demsetz to Grossman–Hart–Moore; the Great Minds volume has no standalone PRT chapter — instead PRT appears inside Williamson's account of TCE's development, as a sibling theory: the Coase theorem, the classical property-rights literature, and two telling encounters with A&D (1972) and GHM (1986).

1. Mahoney (2005), Ch. 3: Property Rights Theory

Mahoney organizes organizational economics into five theories, with property rights among them (behavioral theory, transaction costs, property rights, agency, resource-based/dynamic capabilities). Ch. 3 answers the basic question: How can firms create and allocate wealth?

1.1 Classical PRT: the optimistic efficiency view

  • Initiated by Coase (1960); peaked with Alchian & Demsetz (1973).
  • Demsetz (1967) is the neoclassical exemplar, optimistic that property rights evolve toward efficiency.
  • Three efficiency criteria: universality (all scarce resources are owned), exclusivity (rights are exclusive), transferability (resources move from low- to high-yield uses).

1.2 The critique: efficient evolution often fails

  • Libecap (1989) and North (1990) supply historical accounts challenging the inevitable-evolution view.
  • Mechanism: distributional conflicts create political risk, so politicians propose regulations that do not upset the status quo and offer only limited relief from common-pool losses; regulatory agencies hold similar vested interests.
  • History matters: today's choices are constrained by yesterday's decisions. North is as concerned with institutions that induce stagnation and decline as with successes.

1.3 Other classical strands

  • Eggertsson (1990), three levels: ① property-rights structure modeled but exogenous; ② organizational form endogenous, rights exogenous; ③ social/political rules and political institutions treated as endogenous.
  • Barzel (1989): because measuring all of an asset's attributes is costly, rights are never fully delineated, leaving property open to appropriation (adverse selection, free-riding, shirking).

1.4 Modern PRT: Grossman–Hart–Moore

  • Hart (1990, 1995) joins contractual incompleteness and control to answer: (a) what does ownership mean? (b) what determines firm boundaries? (c) what are the implications of contractual incompleteness? (d) the roles of non-human assets and authority?
  • Ownership = residual rights of control. Buy vs rent: equivalent under zero transaction costs; under positive costs and incomplete contracts the owner decides the unspecified uses, hence has stronger incentives to maintain the machine, learn to operate it, and acquire synergistic assets.
  • Firm boundaries are chosen to allocate control rights optimally; highly complementary assets under different owners → merge to give control to one party.
  • Relation to other theories: principal-agent theory assumes costless complete contracting, so control is irrelevant; TCE is closest to PRT but stresses the costs of writing contracts and pays less attention to how arrangements allocate control rights.

1.5 Forward look

Mahoney predicts that with the growing importance of intellectual property rights in the information age, PRT will receive greater attention in strategic management — a growth area for research.

2. Great Minds (2005): property rights inside Williamson's Ch. 23 (TCE)

Four key passages situate PRT as TCE's sibling:

2.1 The Coase theorem and property-rights assignment

In a regime of zero transaction costs, negotiations would lead to wealth-maximizing arrangements irrespective of the initial assignment of property rights; with positive transaction costs, the assignment matters. (Coase 1960, quoted in Williamson, p. 491)

2.2 The classical PRT literature fed TCE

Williamson recalls his two-semester organization-theory sequence at Penn drawing on his Carnegie background, sociology, "some of the economics of property rights literature", the managerial-discretion literature, and market-failure literature.

2.3 A&D (1972) dismissive of "fiat"

"The famous paper by Armen Alchian and Harold Demsetz (1972)… was dismissive of fiat differences between firm and market." (fn. 15, p. 498)

A direct clash: Williamson holds that the firm has command/fiat and governance the market lacks; A&D hold there is no difference in authority, only team production plus a central contracting party.

2.4 GHM (1986) assumes away governance differences

"The 'property rights theory of the firm' (Grossman and Hart, 1986) assumes away all of these differences." (fn. 16, p. 499)

Williamson's stance: modern PRT (GHM) shares TCE's ancestry but abstracts away the governance-structure differences TCE emphasizes, focusing instead on ex-ante investment incentives and residual control.

3. The two books together

  • Mahoney (2005) gives the panoramic textbook treatment: classical (Coase/Demsetz/A&D/Barzel/Eggertsson) → critique (Libecap/North's "efficient evolution fails") → modern (Hart/GHM's residual control and boundaries).
  • Great Minds (2005) gives the relational placement within TCE's history: the Coase theorem as shared origin, the classical PRT literature feeding TCE, and A&D and GHM challenging or complementing TCE from the "no authority" and "governance assumed away" directions.
  • They are complementary: Mahoney tells what PRT itself is; Great Minds tells how PRT converses with TCE in the organizational-economics map.
