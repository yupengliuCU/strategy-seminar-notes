Allocation of Property Rights and Technological Innovation within Firms

元信息。 作者： Catherine Magelssen（London Business School）
期刊： Strategic Management Journal, Vol. 41, No. 4, pp. 758–787
年份： 2020
DOI： 10.1002/smj.3103
性质： 实证研究（empirical study）——对跨国企业内部产权分配（property-rights allocation）的子公司层面计量分析（工具变量 instrumental variables + 三重差分 triple-difference 设计）
关键词： property rights, innovation, patents, multinational firms, subsidiaries

一句话总结

战略资产（strategic assets）的所有权可以在跨国企业内部被可信地分配：持有这些权利的子公司——同时握有控制权（control rights）与收益权（income rights）——比非所有者产出更多、质量更高的创新，对研发机会（R&D opportunity）的反应也更强烈；而当权利落在用于收入屏蔽（income shielding）的辖区而非专长所在的位置时，要付出实实在在的代价。

这篇论文是课程脉络中指定的实证检验阅读：从经典的产权问题（Alchian & Demsetz 1972：计量 metering 与团队生产 team production），到正式的剩余控制权模型（Grossman & Hart 1986；Hart & Moore 1990），再到其在战略领域的转化（Kim & Mahoney 2005）与所有权能力（ownership competence）视角（Foss et al. 2021）。Magelssen 保留了 Grossman–Hart 的核心对象——资产上剩余权利的分配及其对事前（ex-ante）激励的影响——但把分析单元移到了企业内部：跨国企业中法律上相互独立的子公司。因此，它为下列问题提供了偏向因果的证据：经典产权理论（property-rights theory, PRT）因企业内部契约不可执行而视为不可能的内部权利分配，是否真实存在、是否可信、并是否会改变创新结果。

1. 背景与动机（Background and motivation）

战略理论把战略资产——技术、制造流程、专有知识（know-how）——视为竞争优势的来源（Amit & Schoemaker 1993），并沿 Dunning 的折衷框架（eclectic framework），把它们当作跨国企业可以免费撬动（leverage freely）、在分布于各地的单元之间自由调配的资源。Magelssen 攻击的正是这种"免费撬动"观里隐藏的假设：现实中，跨国企业的确会把战略资产的所有权（ownership rights）分配给特定子公司，方式是法律上相互独立的实体之间签订正式书面契约，并按税法要求把每一年的情况记录在转让定价报告（transfer-pricing reports）中。这种分配是真实的战略选择，因为所有权的两面朝相反方向拉扯：收益权（income rights）让处于低税"收入屏蔽（income-shielding）"辖区的子公司对攫取（appropriation）有价值，而控制权（control rights）让处于专长区域的子公司对创造（creation）有价值。避税研究已经记录第一种动机（Griffith, Miller & O'Connell 2014; Karkinsky & Riedel 2012）；还没有人衡量过牺牲第二种动机会给创新带来什么后果。

理论障碍比"研究空白"更尖锐。产权理论应用于企业之间时预言：资产的拥有者——剩余控制权与剩余收益权的持有者——对提升资产价值有最强的投资激励（Grossman & Hart 1986; Hart & Moore 1990）。然而同一支文献，经 Williamson (1991) 与 Baker, Gibbons & Murphy (1999) 之手，认为企业内部的所有权无法被可信分配：部门或子公司之间的契约在法庭上不可执行，母公司总能推翻单元的控制、重新分配收益，所以内部所有权"不可信、也不应存在"。

"Because the parent’s incentive to intervene is too great, ownership rights to strategic assets within the firm are non-credible and should not exist (Baker et al., 1999)."

>

中文复述：母公司干预的动机过强，因此企业内部对战略资产的所有权不可信、也不应存在（Baker et al. 1999）。

(Introduction, manuscript version)

论文的开场动作是指出：跨国企业是企业内部唯一可能让这种"不可信"论断失效的地方——子公司是独立的法人，第三方——适用 OECD 转让定价指南（OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines）与 IRS Treas. Reg. §1.482 的政府与税务机关——会执行子公司的所有权，并对任何把这些权利从子公司转走的内部操作施加可观的调整成本（adjustment costs）。因此，跨国企业内部的产权起着可信承诺（credible commitments）的作用，问题就变成实证的：这些权利在从事研发（R&D）的子公司之间的分配，会怎样改变它们创新的数量与质量？

2. 核心论点与框架（Core argument / framework）

2.1 产权术语（The property-rights vocabulary）

Magelssen 引入 Grossman–Hart–Fama–Jensen 对资产上两类权利的区分。特定权利（specific rights）使一方有权以约定的收入从事特定活动——在被指派活动的范围内做运营决策。所有权则是其余的一切：就资产的使用、延伸、修改与终止做战略决策的控制权（control rights），以及对交易中未承诺给他人的全部资产回报的收益权。论文的通篇例子把这种区分落到实处：一个对开发手机电池拥有特定权利的实体，可以对电池做运营决策，但未经所有者同意不能把技术转用于电子平板。收益权这一半承担激励工作——

"Whereas non-owners are paid an agreed upon amount for their R&D activities, the owner bears the consequences (responsibility) since it receives the income or loss from any innovations created above the payments promised to the non-owners (Fama and Jensen, 1983)."

>

中文复述：非所有者按约定金额获得其研发活动的报酬；所有者则承担后果（责任）——它在支付了承诺给非所有者的报酬之后，收取任何新创造出来的创新的收益或亏损（Fama and Jensen 1983）。

(Theoretical Background — Property rights, manuscript version)

2.2 所有权为何能在跨国企业内部存在并具有约束力（Why ownership rights can exist and bind inside the MNE）

程式化例子是 ABC Transmissions：德国子公司持有变速箱技术的所有权，自己从事研发，并以成本加成 15%（a 15 percent mark-up on costs）内部签约让英国子公司承担研发。无论英国子公司发明什么，契约都把权利（title）归于德国；项目若失败，英国仍收取加成，德国记下亏损；若出现爆款，德国保留超出承诺支付额之上的收益。论文附录重现了一份真实内部研发契约中正是这一类的条款：子公司 B"应及时着手每个项目……并应尽最大努力按[子公司 A]的规格与指示完成项目"，按月汇报，并"承认[子公司 A]对无形财产（Intangible Property）拥有排他性权利、所有权与利益"。这些契约之所以镜像产权概念，是因为政府要求如此：转让定价法（transfer-pricing law）强制跨国企业为税务目的指定哪个实体在经济上拥有每项无形资产。于是所有权把战略控制与业绩后果，从仅仅执行活动的子公司，移到持有权利的子公司身上。

"Governments uphold subsidiary ownership rights and impose significant adjustment costs on MNEs to transfer ownership rights away from subsidiaries (e.g. OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines "OECD TPG"; IRS Treas. Reg. §1.482). Thus, ownership rights may serve as credible commitments to delegating control and income rights to subsidiaries."

>

中文复述：政府支持子公司的所有权，并对跨国企业把所有权从子公司转走施加可观的调整成本（如 OECD 转让定价指南 "OECD TPG"；IRS Treas. Reg. §1.482）。因此，所有权可作为把控制权与收益权下放给子公司的可信承诺。

(Introduction, manuscript version)

2.3 作为既定方案的对照目标：自主权与社会化（The target: autonomy-and-socialization as the received solution）

国际管理文献用两种工具管理子公司的研发：决策权（decision-making authority）的下放（自主权 autonomy）与社会化（socialization）。Magelssen 认为相对所有权，两者都不完整。没有收益权的下放对"谁获得回报、谁承担成本"保持沉默，因此母公司隐性地吸收后果，代理问题（agency problem）出现：自主的子公司可以挑选带来私人收益的偏好项目；因为比委托人更厌恶风险而选择稳妥下注；或因为转移知识的成本高而囤积想法（Hoenen & Kostova 2015; Fey & Furu 2008）。母公司以撤销授权相威胁的退路也站不住，论文给出三点理由：可预期的干预本身就会压制子公司对新项目的搜寻（Aghion & Tirole 1994; Baker et al. 1999）；橡皮图章式的母公司只在出现严重负面结果后才撤销授权，这诱使子公司去赌私人收益大、后果大的项目；想法不可观察，母公司无法判断子公司是否在真正执行这些想法。社会化的实证表现同样不佳：研究发现跨国企业实际上并不用社会化来控制自主的研发子公司（Ambos & Schlegelmilch 2007; Asakawa 1996），而且在卓越中心（centers of excellence），社会化与技术成功负相关（Ambos & Reitsperger 2004）——因为它培育出以总部为中心的文化，阻碍吸收本地知识（Frost 2001）。

2.4 独特机制：情境责任（The distinct mechanism: situated responsibility）

论文对机制之争的贡献是：所有权通过代理视角忽略的一个渠道起作用——不只是跨越母子公司间距离的激励对齐，而是决策控制与收入敞口（income exposure）的共置（co-location），它产生 Magelssen 所称的情境责任（situated responsibility，情境责任）。由于作为所有者的子公司承担战略资产的收益或亏损，企业能够追踪其业绩并让其负责；由于它也控制研究方向，它可以在不把每个决策都上报总部的情况下，对本地知识采取行动。

"Co-locating control rights with income rights motivate self-monitoring (Jensen and Meckling, 1992). When decision control and income rights are held by the same entity, the agency problem of goal misalignment goes away."

>

中文复述：把控制权与收益权共置会激发自我监督（Jensen and Meckling 1992）。当决策控制与收益权由同一实体持有时，目标错位的代理问题就消失了。

(Theoretical Background — Management of international R&D, manuscript version)

这些机制有现实咬合力。控制权砍掉了创新所需的沟通与审批成本——当想法是"粘性（sticky）"的（von Hippel 1994）、难以隔空解释时（Prahalad & Doz 1981）；收益权使所有者成为成功想法的剩余索取者（residual claimant），因此它搜寻得更用力，而无法捕获回报的非所有者几乎没有理由提出想法，项目失败时还能归咎于所有者。Magelssen 用一个日本子公司举例：它拥有每年产生 $80 million 收入的产品技术；价值提高 10% 会把它管理的资金池扩到 $88 million，可供它跨项目再投资——所有权让受风险的资源随子公司自身的成功而放大。

2.5 配置权衡与三个假设（The allocation trade-off and the three hypotheses）

因为所有权一旦授予就难以迁移（政府要求内部转移按净现值 net-present value 补偿），事前（ex-ante）的地点选择在战略上具有决定性。两类地点属性吸引权利：创新潜力（innovation potential）——区域专长加上东道国的研发激励（税收补贴 tax subsidies）——以及收入屏蔽潜力（income-shielding potential）——低税辖区。母公司在控制、创新与攫取之间权衡。这给出三个假设：所有者比非所有者申请更多专利（H1）；当研发机会上升时，所有者的产出增幅大于非所有者（H2）——因为机会只有在子公司既感知到本地信号、又控制研究方向时才可采取行动；所有者和非所有者之间的反应差距在相对技术优势较强（高 RTA，relative technological advantage）的地区扩大（H3）——那里外部知识流使"由专家控制"格外有价值。ABB–Westinghouse 轶事承载 H3：被收购的 Westinghouse 继电器单元此前无法说服母公司资助固态微处理器继电器；一旦 ABB 给了它项目选择与业绩责任，它立刻选择了那个方向——及时挡住了新的竞争对手。

3. 证据与例子（Evidence / examples）

3.1 情境与数据（Setting and data）

实证情境是一个新的、保密的子公司层面数据集。所有权信息来自一家咨询公司跨国企业客户的转让定价报告——OECD 转让定价指南要求跨国企业每年编制的文件——它们明确说明哪些实体拥有企业无形资产的经济所有权。专利数据来自 USPTO（按发明人城市/州/国家匹配到子公司、按申请日匹配到年份）；RTA 与研发税收补贴数据来自 OECD；市场集中度数据来自 Compustat Global Vantage；跨国企业财务数据来自 Orbis。分析在子公司层面进行：起始样本是 78 家跨国企业、1,134 家从事研发的子公司、1997–2011 年共 4,686 个子公司-年度观测；剔除被许可的权利（licensed rights）并合并控制变量后，缩减为 952 家子公司的 3,789 个观测；再剔除专利数为零的两位数行业后，为 942 家子公司的 3,753 个观测。数据集覆盖 50,934 项已授权专利，其中 29,028 项只有单一发明人，10,711 项的多位发明人位于同一子公司所在地，11,195 项横跨多个地点。样本在从事研发的外国子公司占比（16.9 vs. 18.2 percent）与每年专利数（16.10 vs. 18.65，p < 0.29）上近似于 Berry (2014)。从事研发的子公司中，13.3% 持有所有权，86.7% 是受所有者签约的非所有者。论文 Table 1 显示所有者同时聚集在避税天堂（tax havens：爱尔兰占所有者观测的 7.88 percent、瑞士 8.52、新加坡 2.09）与高 RTA、高税的核心区（美国 30.87、英国 13.34、德国 8.20、法国 4.98）——原始数据里"创造 vs. 屏蔽"权衡的可见证据。

3.3 识别策略（Identification strategy）

所有权并非随机分配，论文对此直言不讳：提升创新的不可观测因素也可能提高子公司获得权利的概率，因果还可能从能力反向跑到所有权，使 OLS 上偏。识别因此依靠两个设计。其一，两阶段最小二乘（two-stage least squares, TSLS）模型用避税天堂（Tax Haven）作为战略资产所有权的工具变量（instrumental variable）——一个东道国哑变量，应当通过收入屏蔽动机预测所有权，但不应直接影响创新。第一阶段很强：位于避税天堂使持有所有权的概率提高 29.2%（系数 0.292，Table 4 第 2 列），研发税收补贴为正且显著，Kleibergen-Paap Wald F 统计量为 22.52，可排除弱工具问题。其二，三重差分（triple-difference, DDD）设计利用错开的、外生的研发机会冲击——各东道国在不同时点提高研发税收补贴——询问在所属国补贴提高后，所有者的创新是否相对非所有者上升，并以未受处理国家中所有者–非所有者差距作为反事实。DDD 通过子公司固定效应（fixed effects）、年份固定效应与所有者×年份固定效应（owner-by-year fixed effects），差分掉底层的所有者–非所有者差异（因此"权利被分配给更具创新力的子公司"这一点被差分消除），标准误在子公司层面聚类。事前趋势检验（Figure 2）显示，补贴变动前所有者–非所有者的相对趋势与零在统计上无差异。

3.5 对实证部分的评述（Commentary on the empirics）

用一句话概括：这是一个子公司-年度面板，处理变量是一个二元指标——是否正式拥有任一战略无形资产（从保密转让定价报告编码而来），结果变量是 USPTO 专利数与五年内前向引用数，识别来自避税天堂工具（TSLS）与错开的东道国研发税收补贴提高（DDD，含子公司、年份与所有者×年份固定效应）。对研究权利分配的战略文献而言，这一设计非同寻常地强——它把经过验证、有行政记录的处理变量与两种不同的识别策略加一个外生冲击配对——但有四个威胁值得细察。

威胁 1——进入所有权的选择与工具的局限（selection into ownership and the limits of the instrument）。 持有权利的子公司恰恰是母公司根据创新与税收指向而选定的；作者明确承认由此产生的内生性与反向因果。TSLS 的回应完全押注在排他性约束（exclusion restriction）上：避税天堂区位只通过所有权影响创新。该约束合理但不可检验，而且它因如下事实而紧绷：位于避税天堂的"所有者"往往是低活动量的实体，主要职能是把收入入账——这类实体在不可观测维度上很可能不同于真正的研发所有者，差异超出权利哑变量本身。DDD 是更可信的一条腿，因为它差分掉任何永久性的所有者–非所有者差异。但所有权几乎不随时间变化——作者报告"所有权变动只有少量几次"——所以子公司固定效应吸收掉大部分处理变异，DDD 实际上靠所有者 × 补贴变化交互来识别；基于所有权变动（再分配事件）的设计会是更强的因果检验。相关地，DDD 只在改变补贴制度的国家上有识别力；从未改变的国家（包括多数避税天堂）只充当反事实对照。

威胁 2——处理变量的度量（measurement of the treatment）。 所有权是一个"持有任一战略资产"的二元旗标，由转让定价文件编码。"经济所有权"在这些报告里是一个税务分配构念：OECD 要求企业指定税务上应当获得回报的实体，这与研发决策的事实控制不必一致。只拥有一件商标的子公司与拥有公司核心技术的子公司得分相同；部分、联合与分层（tiered）所有权结构坍缩为同一类别；由一名研究员对保密文件编码，也不可独立复现。若所有权旗标含噪声，效应被衰减；若它系统性地标记了同时具备其他治理特征的子公司（如独立董事会、完整损益授权），系数可能捕获一揽子"独立主体地位"，而非权利本身。

威胁 3——结果变量的度量（measurement of the outcome）。 专利只捕捉编码化的、可专利的发明。申请速率对战略动机有反应（防御性申请、奖励专利数量的税收抵免规则）；当研发由一家子公司执行、权利由另一家持有时，发明人所在地匹配可能错置发明；基于引用的质量在 2009 年截断。Table 8 中研发支出的零结果削弱了"所有者只是拿到更多资源"的说法，但也与如下情形一致：所有者对补贴变化的反应是更多改标签或申请，而非更多发明——论文的解释（项目选择）只是对一个无法区分真实发明与报告发明之结果的一种解读。

威胁 4——外部效度（external validity）。 样本是一家转让定价咨询公司的客户名单（102 家数据完整的跨国企业；其中 78 家有申请专利的研发子公司），偏重制造业，观测期为 1997–2011，专利只在 USPTO 度量。在 USPTO 申请专利的外国子公司是一个被选择的总体；而且执行环境（转让定价审查）与税收制度此后都已变化。能强化因果识别的是：把所有权变动事件当作准实验（内部重组、法院裁决、或 OECD 监管转向——如迫使权利遣返的税基侵蚀规则 base-erosion rules），对"涉及哪些资产、利润份额多大"的更丰富连续度量，直接度量决策权与研发项目结果以分离控制渠道与收益渠道，以及非专利的创新结果。

4. 在文献中的位置（Position in the literature）

论文位于三支文献的交汇处，其在产权脉络中的位置是精确的。它接续现代产权理论研究纲领——Grossman & Hart (1986) 与 Hart & Moore (1990)，综述见 Hart (1995)——其核心是：把资产剩余控制权分配给事前投资最重要的一方会提高总价值。Magelssen 追问：当"缔约方"是一家企业的子公司时，这一逻辑意味着什么。在这样做时，它直面 Williamson (1991) 与 Baker, Gibbons & Murphy (1999) 的经典反驳：企业内部的权利不可能可信，因为科层总能推翻它们；Magelssen 的回答是：跨国企业子公司是例外，因为第三方（执行转让定价规则的政府）提供了内部科层缺乏的可信性。她对收益权与剩余索取权（residual claims）作为所有权中承担责任的半边（responsibility-bearing half）的强调，上溯到更早的产权/监督传统——Demsetz (1967)、Alchian & Demsetz (1972)、Fama & Jensen (1983)——以及 Jensen & Meckling (1992) 的命题：把决策权与收益权共置会产生自我监督（self-monitoring）。因此这篇论文不是新的正式模型，而是对"现代产权理论机制是否在企业边界内部运行"的实证检验。

相对产权理论的战略应用，Magelssen 把 Lerner & Merges (1998)、Elfenbein & Lerner (2003)、Oxley (1999) 与 Leiponen (2008) 的跨企业实证工作——都研究联盟或契约边界上的权利分配——向内延伸到跨国企业内部契约。它还在子公司层面操作化了 Kim & Mahoney (2005) 把产权经济学接上资源基础观（resource-based view）时在概念上发展的逻辑；并回应所有权能力脉络（Foss、Klein 及其合作者；参见 Foss et al. 2021）——它显示权利相对能力的位置——谁拥有创新者需要的东西——能预测谁在创新。针对国际商务文献，论文的靶子是下放与社会化正典（Ghoshal & Bartlett 1988; Nobel & Birkinshaw 1998; Mudambi, Mudambi & Navarra 2007）：那支文献用自主权加公司文化来管理子公司主动性（subsidiary initiative），Magelssen 则论证收益权维度是缺失的变量，并以 Ambos & Schlegelmilch (2007)、Asakawa (1996) 与 Ambos & Reitsperger (2004) 为证：仅靠社会化并未奏效。最后，它把一支把所有权放置纯粹当作利润转移（profit-shifting）的税收文献（Griffith et al. 2014; Karkinsky & Riedel 2012; Dharmapala & Hines 2009）转化为战略性的——为税收动机的配置标出创新成本。在课程脉络内，这篇论文是实证的收官之作：检验 Alchian–Demsetz 与 Grossman–Hart 形式化的剩余权利逻辑，在接触真实企业边界后是否仍然成立。

5. 局限（Limitations）

(a) 作者自述的局限（The author's own stated limitations）

  • 专利是唯一的创新度量： 论文"the analyses rely on patents as a measure of innovation"——分析以专利作为创新的度量——因此对其他创新类型（产品、流程、服务）的推广留待将来。
  • 没有对权衡做福利核算： 把权利配置给价值创造、价值攫取与控制各自的相对损益，都"outside the scope of this study"（不在本研究的范围内）。
  • 权利位置的简化模型： 第一阶段对"所有权放在哪里"的选择处理得很简约；更丰富的模型应允许跨国企业各区位选择之间的相互依赖。
  • 所有权变动很少： "the sample has only a small number of changes in ownership"——样本中所有权变动只有少量几次——因此要识别改变权利配置的后果，需要更大、变动更多的样本。

(b) 独立判断（Independent judgment）

  • [论文主张（paper claim）] 论文声称"subsidiary strategic asset ownership rights are a commitment mechanism that, beyond delegating decision making authority, credibly allocates control over the strategic asset and its income"——子公司战略资产所有权是一种承诺机制：在授权决策权之外，它把对战略资产及其收益的控制可信地分配出去。该主张是明说的，它的真实性使整个设计能被解读为权利在做因果工作。
  • [证据（evidence）] 支撑证据是制度记录——OECD 转让定价指南与 IRS 法规要求内部转让按净现值补偿——加上附录里显示权利被书面化并执行的真实契约条款。这是"跨国企业内部权利带第三方牙齿"的真实证据，尽管它记录的所有权更多是税务法律构念，而非事实上的管理控制。
  • [推断（inference）] 二元所有权度量几乎肯定低估了权利的经济异质性：拥有一件次要商标的子公司与拥有公司核心平台技术的子公司都得 1 分；分级所有权、联合所有权或只覆盖资产部分寿命的权利不可见。若理论讲的是剩余索取权的强度，那么处理变量含度量误差，效应很可能被衰减。
  • [推断（inference）] 渠道分解未经检验：控制权与收益权在所有权变量中、在理论中都捆绑在一起，因此 DDD 无法说明对研发机会冲击的反应来自更快的审批、更强的动机、还是两者兼有。论文自己的 Table 8（研发支出无差异）支持"项目选择"的故事，但要孤立出情境责任这一机制，需要固定控制权、变动收益权（或反之）的设计。
  • [开放问题（open question）] 效应反映的是真实发明还是专利归属与申请行为，仍未解决：因为权利决定哪个实体在法上拥有发明人的创造，一次所有权变动可以把专利数在子公司之间搬移，而底层的发明活动并未移动。区分"真实发明"与"被重新归置的发明"是论文最深层的未决问题，而作者自己"所有权变动很少"的局限意味着数据尚不能回答它。

6. 结论（Conclusion）

Magelssen 确立了三件事。第一，理论主张——企业内部的所有权不可信——恰恰在企业被组织为法律上独立的子公司之处失效：转让定价法使内部权利分配可执行、且逆转代价高昂，因此产权是跨国企业内部的真实治理工具。第二，在证据上，持有这些权利的子公司产出更多专利、更多带外部引用的专利，而且——最有说服力的是——把外生的研发机会改善转化为创新，而非所有者不会；差距集中在技术优势地区——那里"由专家控制"应当重要。第三，税收规避型的权利配置有可识别的战略成本：当所有权落在收入屏蔽地区，最有条件创新的研发单元产出的创新更少、影响力更低。对战略的一行总结是：无形资产上产权的位置不是税收后台的细节，而是关于"企业内部谁将为技术轨迹承担责任、并因此塑造它"的一阶决策。

Allocation of Property Rights and Technological Innovation within Firms

Authors: Catherine Magelssen (London Business School)
Journal: Strategic Management Journal, Vol. 41, No. 4, pp. 758–787
Year: 2020
DOI: 10.1002/smj.3103
Nature: empirical study — subsidiary-level econometric analysis (instrumental variables + triple-difference designs) of property-rights allocation within multinational firms
Keywords: property rights, innovation, patents, multinational firms, subsidiaries

One-sentence summary

Ownership rights over strategic assets can be credibly allocated inside multinational firms, and subsidiaries that hold them — both control and income rights — produce more and higher-quality innovations and respond more strongly to R&D opportunity than non-owners, at a real cost when rights sit in tax-shielding rather than expertise locations.

Reading note. This paper is the assigned empirical test in the course arc that runs from the classical property-rights problem (Alchian & Demsetz 1972: metering and team production) through the formal residual-control-rights model (Grossman & Hart 1986; Hart & Moore 1990), its translation into strategy (Kim & Mahoney 2005), and the ownership-competence view (Foss et al. 2021). Magelssen keeps Grossman–Hart's core object — the allocation of residual rights over an asset and its effect on ex-ante incentives — but moves the unit of analysis inside the firm, to legally separate subsidiaries of a multinational. It therefore supplies causal-leaning evidence on whether the internal rights allocation that classical PRT treats as impossible (because intra-firm contracts are unenforceable) actually exists, is credible, and changes innovation outcomes.

1. Background and motivation

Strategy theory treats strategic assets — technologies, manufacturing processes, know-how — as the source of competitive advantage (Amit & Schoemaker 1993) and, following Dunning's eclectic framework, as resources that an MNE can leverage freely across its geographically dispersed units. Magelssen attacks the hidden assumption in that "free leveraging" view: in practice, MNEs do allocate ownership rights over strategic assets to particular subsidiaries, and they do it through formal written contracts between legally separate entities, documented each year in transfer-pricing reports required by tax law. The allocation is a genuine strategic choice because the two faces of ownership pull in opposite directions: income rights make a subsidiary in a low-tax "income-shielding" location valuable for appropriation, while control rights make a subsidiary in a region of expertise valuable for creation. Tax-avoidance research had documented the first motive (Griffith, Miller & O'Connell 2014; Karkinsky & Riedel 2012); nobody had measured the innovation consequences of the second motive being sacrificed.

The theoretical obstacle is sharper than a research gap. Property-rights theory, applied between firms, predicts that the owner of an asset — the holder of residual control and residual income rights — has the strongest incentive to invest in the asset's value (Grossman & Hart 1986; Hart & Moore 1990). Yet the same literature, in Williamson's (1991) and Baker, Gibbons & Murphy's (1999) hands, held that ownership rights within a firm cannot be credibly allocated: contracts between divisions or subsidiaries are not enforceable in courts, and the parent can always override unit control and reallocate income, so internal ownership rights "are non-credible and should not exist."

"Because the parent’s incentive to intervene is too great, ownership rights to strategic assets within the firm are non-credible and should not exist (Baker et al., 1999)."
(Introduction, manuscript version)

The paper's opening move is to notice that MNEs are the one place inside firms where this non-credibility claim can fail: subsidiaries are separate legal persons, and third parties — governments and tax authorities applying the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and IRS Treas. Reg. §1.482 — enforce subsidiary ownership rights and impose significant adjustment costs on any internal transfer of those rights away from the subsidiary. Intra-MNE ownership rights therefore function as credible commitments, and the question becomes empirical: does the allocation of these rights across R&D subsidiaries change how much and how well they innovate?

2. Core argument / framework

2.1 The property-rights vocabulary

Magelssen imports the Grossman–Hart–Fama–Jensen distinction between two kinds of rights over an asset. Specific rights entitle a party to perform specified activities for an agreed income — making operational decisions inside the scope of the assigned activity. Ownership rights are everything else: the control rights to make strategic decisions over the use, extension, modification, and termination of the asset, and the income rights to all returns from the asset not promised to others in the exchange. The paper's running example makes the split concrete: an entity with specific rights to develop a cell-phone battery can make operational decisions about the battery, but cannot redirect the technology to electronic tablets without the owner's approval. The income-rights half does the incentive work —

"Whereas non-owners are paid an agreed upon amount for their R&D activities, the owner bears the consequences (responsibility) since it receives the income or loss from any innovations created above the payments promised to the non-owners (Fama and Jensen, 1983)."
(Theoretical Background — Property rights, manuscript version)

2.2 Why ownership rights can exist and bind inside the MNE

The stylized example is ABC Transmissions: the German subsidiary holds ownership rights to a transmission technology, performs R&D itself, and internally contracts the U.K. subsidiary to do R&D for a 15 percent mark-up on costs. Whatever the U.K. subsidiary invents, the contract assigns title to Germany; if the project fails, the U.K. still collects its mark-up and Germany books the loss; if a blockbuster emerges, Germany keeps the income above the promised payments. The appendix of the paper reproduces clauses from a real internal R&D contract of exactly this kind: subsidiary B "shall promptly undertake each Project…and shall use its best efforts to complete the Project in accordance with the specifications and directions of [Subsidiary A]," reports monthly, and "acknowledges [Subsidiary A]'s exclusive right, title, and interest in and to the Intangible Property." These contracts mirror property-rights concepts because governments require them: transfer-pricing law obliges MNEs to designate, for tax purposes, which entity economically owns each intangible. Ownership thus shifts both strategic control and performance consequences from the subsidiary that merely performs activities to the subsidiary that holds the rights.

"Governments uphold subsidiary ownership rights and impose significant adjustment costs on MNEs to transfer ownership rights away from subsidiaries (e.g. OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines "OECD TPG"; IRS Treas. Reg. §1.482). Thus, ownership rights may serve as credible commitments to delegating control and income rights to subsidiaries."
(Introduction, manuscript version)

2.3 The target: autonomy-and-socialization as the received solution

The international-management literature managed subsidiary R&D through two instruments: delegation of decision-making authority (autonomy) and socialization. Magelssen argues both are incomplete relative to ownership rights. Delegation without income rights is silent about who reaps returns and bears costs, so the parent implicitly absorbs the consequences and an agency problem appears: an autonomous subsidiary can pick pet projects with private benefits, choose safe bets because it is more risk-averse than the principal, or hoard ideas because transferring knowledge is costly (Hoenen & Kostova 2015; Fey & Furu 2008). The parent's fallback — threatening to revoke authority — is weak for three reasons the paper gives: anticipated intervention itself suppresses the subsidiary's search for new projects (Aghion & Tirole 1994; Baker et al. 1999); rubber-stamping parents only revoke after severe negative outcomes, which tempts irresponsible bets with large private benefits; and because ideas are unobservable, the parent cannot tell whether the subsidiary is acting on them. Socialization fares no better empirically: studies find MNEs do not actually use socialization to control autonomous R&D subsidiaries (Ambos & Schlegelmilch 2007; Asakawa 1996), and socialization is negatively associated with technological success at centers of excellence (Ambos & Reitsperger 2004) because it breeds a headquarter-centric culture that blocks absorption of local knowledge (Frost 2001).

2.4 The distinct mechanism: situated responsibility

The paper's contribution to the mechanism debate is that ownership rights work through a channel the agency view misses — not just incentive alignment across a parent–subsidiary gap, but the co-location of decision control with income exposure, which produces what Magelssen calls situated responsibility (情境责任). Because the owner subsidiary bears the income or loss of the strategic asset, the firm can track its performance and hold it accountable; because it also controls research direction, it can act on local knowledge without routing every decision through headquarters.

"Co-locating control rights with income rights motivate self-monitoring (Jensen and Meckling, 1992). When decision control and income rights are held by the same entity, the agency problem of goal misalignment goes away."
(Theoretical Background — Management of international R&D, manuscript version)

The mechanisms have practical teeth. Control rights cut the communication and approval costs that innovation requires when ideas are "sticky" (von Hippel 1994) and hard to explain across distance (Prahalad & Doz 1981); income rights make the owner the residual claimant on successful ideas, so it searches harder, while a non-owner who cannot capture returns has little reason to raise ideas at all and can blame the owner when projects fail. Magelssen illustrates with a Japanese subsidiary that owns product technology generating $80 million of income per year: a ten percent value increase raises the pool it manages to $88 million, which it can reinvest across projects — ownership scales the resources at stake with the subsidiary's own success.

2.5 The allocation trade-off and the three hypotheses

Because ownership rights are costly to relocate once granted (governments demand compensation at net-present value for internal transfers), the ex-ante location choice is strategically decisive. Two location attributes attract rights: innovation potential — regional expertise plus host-country R&D incentives (tax subsidies) — and income-shielding potential — low-tax jurisdictions. Parents trade off control, innovation, and appropriation. This yields three hypotheses: owners patent more than non-owners (H1); owners increase output more than non-owners when R&D opportunity rises (H2), because opportunity is only actionable if the subsidiary both perceives local signals and controls research direction; and the owner–non-owner response gap widens in relatively technologically advantaged (high-RTA) locations (H3), where the flow of external knowledge makes control-by-the-expert especially valuable. The ABB–Westinghouse anecdote carries H3: the acquired Westinghouse relays unit, previously unable to persuade its parent to fund solid-state microprocessor relays, immediately chose that direction once ABB gave it project selection and performance responsibility — in time to fend off a new competitor.

3. Evidence / examples

3.1 Setting and data

The empirical setting is a new, confidential subsidiary-level dataset. Ownership-rights information comes from the transfer-pricing reports of a consulting firm's MNE clients — documents MNEs must produce each year under OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines — which explicitly state which entities hold economic ownership of the firm's intangibles. Patent data come from the USPTO (matched to subsidiaries by inventor city/state/country and to years by filing date); RTA and R&D-tax-subsidy data come from the OECD; market-concentration data from Compustat Global Vantage; MNE financials from Orbis. The analysis is at the subsidiary level: a starting sample of 4,686 subsidiary-year observations on 1,134 R&D-performing subsidiaries of 78 MNEs, 1997–2011, reduced to 3,789 observations on 952 subsidiaries after excluding licensed rights and merging controls, and to 3,753 observations on 942 subsidiaries once two-digit industries with zero patenting are dropped. The dataset spans 50,934 granted patents, of which 29,028 have a single inventor, 10,711 have multiple inventors in one subsidiary location, and 11,195 span several locations. The sample resembles Berry's (2014) in the share of foreign subsidiaries doing R&D (16.9 vs. 18.2 percent) and patents per year (16.10 vs. 18.65, p < 0.29). Among R&D subsidiaries, 13.3 percent hold ownership rights and 86.7 percent are non-owners contracted by owners. Table 1 of the manuscript shows owners clustered both in tax havens (Ireland 7.88 percent of owner-observations, Switzerland 8.52, Singapore 2.09) and in high-RTA, high-tax cores (United States 30.87, United Kingdom 13.34, Germany 8.20, France 4.98) — visible evidence of the creation-versus-shielding trade-off in the raw data.

3.3 Identification strategy

Ownership rights are not randomly assigned, and the paper says so plainly: unobservables that raise innovation may also raise the chance a subsidiary gets rights, and the causation could run backward from capability to ownership, biasing OLS upward. Identification therefore rests on two designs. First, a two-stage least squares (TSLS) model instruments strategic asset ownership with Tax Haven — a host-country dummy that should predict ownership (through the income-shielding motive) but not affect innovation directly. The first stage is strong: tax haven raises the probability of holding ownership rights by 29.2 percent (coefficient 0.292, column 2 of Table 4), and R&D tax subsidies are positive and significant, with a Kleibergen-Paap Wald F statistic of 22.52 against weak instruments. Second, a triple-difference (DDD) design exploits staggered, exogenous shocks to R&D opportunity — host countries raised R&D tax subsidies at different times — and asks whether owners' innovation rises relative to non-owners' after their country's subsidy increase, using the owner–non-owner gap in untreated countries as the counterfactual. The DDD controls for the underlying owner–non-owner difference (so assignment of rights to more innovative subsidiaries is differenced out) through subsidiary fixed effects, year fixed effects, and owner-by-year fixed effects, with standard errors clustered at the subsidiary. Pre-trend tests (Figure 2) show owner–non-owner relative trends before the subsidy changes are statistically indistinguishable from zero.

3.5 Commentary on the empirics

This is, in one sentence, a subsidiary-year panel in which the treatment is a binary indicator of formal ownership of any strategic intangible (coded from confidential transfer-pricing reports), the outcomes are USPTO patent counts and five-year forward citations, and identification comes from a tax-haven instrument (TSLS) and from staggered host-country R&D-tax-subsidy increases (DDD with subsidiary, year, and owner-year fixed effects). The design is unusually strong for strategy research on rights allocation — it pairs a validated, administratively documented treatment with two distinct identification strategies and an exogenous shock — but four threats deserve scrutiny.

Threat 1 — selection into ownership and the limits of the instrument. The subsidiary that holds rights is chosen by the parent precisely because of where innovation and taxes point; the author concedes the resulting endogeneity and reverse causality explicitly. The TSLS response rests entirely on the exclusion restriction that tax-haven location affects innovation only through ownership rights. That restriction is plausible but not testable, and it is strained by the fact that haven-located "owners" are often low-activity entities whose main function is booking income — entities that plausibly differ from genuine R&D owners on unobservables beyond the rights dummy. The DDD is the more credible leg because it differences out any permanent owner–non-owner difference. But ownership is nearly time-invariant — the author reports "only a small number of changes in ownership" — so subsidiary fixed effects absorb most of the treatment variation, and the DDD effectively identifies off owner × subsidy-change interactions; an ownership change design (reallocation events) would be the stronger causal test. Relatedly, the DDD is identified on countries that changed subsidy regimes; countries that never changed (including most havens) serve only as counterfactual controls.

Threat 2 — measurement of the treatment. Ownership is a binary "holds any strategic asset" flag coded from transfer-pricing documents. "Economic ownership" in these reports is a tax-allocation construct: the OECD requires firms to designate the entity that should receive the returns for tax purposes, which need not coincide with de facto control over research decisions. A one-trademark owner and an owner of the firm's core technology score identically; partial, joint, and tiered ownership structures collapse into one category; and a single researcher coding confidential documents is not independently replicable. If the ownership flag is noisy, effects are attenuated; if it systematically tags subsidiaries that also have other governance features (e.g., separate boards, full profit-and-loss mandates), the coefficient may capture a bundle of "standalone status" rather than rights per se.

Threat 3 — measurement of the outcome. Patents capture codified, patentable invention. Filing rates respond to strategic motives (defensive filing, tax-credit rules that reward patent counts), the inventor-location matching may misattribute inventions when R&D is performed by one subsidiary and rights held by another, and citation-based quality is censored at 2009. Table 8's null result for R&D spending cuts against a pure "owners get more resources" story but is also consistent with owners responding to subsidy changes by re-labeling or filing more rather than inventing more — the paper's interpretation (project selection) is one reading of a result that cannot distinguish real from reported invention.

Threat 4 — external validity. The sample is the client list of one transfer-pricing consulting firm (102 MNEs with full data; 78 with patenting R&D subsidiaries), weighted toward manufacturing, observed 1997–2011, with patents measured only at the USPTO. Foreign subsidiaries patenting at the USPTO are a selected population, and both the enforcement environment (transfer-pricing scrutiny) and tax regimes have changed since. What would strengthen causal identification: ownership-change events as quasi-experiments (internal restructurings, court rulings, or OECD regulatory shifts such as the base-erosion rules that forced rights repatriation), richer continuous measures of which assets and what share of profits are at stake, direct data on decision rights and R&D project outcomes to separate the control and income channels, and non-patent innovation outcomes.

4. Position in the literature

The paper sits at the intersection of three literatures, and its position in the property-rights line is precise. It takes the modern PRT research program — Grossman & Hart (1986) and Hart & Moore (1990), summarized in Hart (1995) — in which allocating residual control rights over an asset to the party whose ex-ante investment matters most raises total value, and asks what that logic implies when the "contracting parties" are subsidiaries of one firm. In so doing it confronts the classical objection, from Williamson (1991) and Baker, Gibbons & Murphy (1999), that intra-firm rights cannot be credible because the hierarchy can always override them; Magelssen's answer is that MNE subsidiaries are exceptional because third parties (governments enforcing transfer-pricing rules) supply the credibility the internal hierarchy lacks. Her emphasis on income rights and residual claims as the responsibility-bearing half of ownership reaches back to the earlier property-rights/monitoring tradition — Demsetz (1967), Alchian & Demsetz (1972), Fama & Jensen (1983) — and to Jensen & Meckling's (1992) proposition that co-locating decision and income rights produces self-monitoring. The paper is therefore not a new formal model but the empirical test of whether the modern PRT mechanism operates inside firm boundaries.

Relative to the strategy applications of PRT, Magelssen extends the inter-firm empirical work of Lerner & Merges (1998), Elfenbein & Lerner (2003), Oxley (1999), and Leiponen (2008) — all of whom study rights allocation across alliance or contract boundaries — inward to intra-MNE contracts. It also operationalizes, at subsidiary level, the logic that Kim & Mahoney (2005) developed conceptually when they connected property-rights economics to the resource-based view, and it speaks to the ownership-competence strand (Foss, Klein and colleagues; cf. Foss et al. 2021) by showing that the location of rights relative to competence — who owns what the innovators need — predicts who innovates. Against the international-business literature, the paper's foil is the delegation-and-socialization canon (Ghoshal & Bartlett 1988; Nobel & Birkinshaw 1998; Mudambi, Mudambi & Navarra 2007): where that literature manages subsidiary initiative with autonomy plus corporate culture, Magelssen argues the income-rights dimension was the missing variable, and she marshals Ambos & Schlegelmilch (2007), Asakawa (1996), and Ambos & Reitsperger (2004) as evidence that socialization alone has not worked. Finally, it converts a tax literature that treated ownership placement purely as profit-shifting (Griffith et al. 2014; Karkinsky & Riedel 2012; Dharmapala & Hines 2009) into a strategic one by pricing the innovation cost of tax-motivated allocation. Within the course's arc, this paper is the empirical capstone: it tests whether the residual-rights logic that Alchian–Demsetz and Grossman–Hart formalized survives contact with actual corporate boundaries.

5. Limitations

(a) The author's own stated limitations

  • Patents as the only innovation measure: "the analyses rely on patents as a measure of innovation," so extensions to other innovation types (products, processes, services) are left open.
  • No welfare accounting of the trade-off: the relative gains and losses of allocating rights for value creation versus value appropriation versus control are "outside the scope of this study."
  • A simple model of rights location: the first stage treats the choice of where to locate ownership rights parsimoniously; richer models should allow interdependence among the MNE's location choices.
  • Few ownership changes: "the sample has only a small number of changes in ownership," so the consequences of changing rights allocation need larger, change-rich samples to identify.

(b) Independent judgment

  • [paper claim] The paper claims that "subsidiary strategic asset ownership rights are a commitment mechanism that, beyond delegating decision making authority, credibly allocates control over the strategic asset and its income." That claim is explicit, and its truth is what makes the whole design interpretable as rights doing causal work.
  • [evidence] The supporting evidence is the institutional record — OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and IRS regulations requiring compensation at net present value for internal transfers — plus the appendix's real contract clauses showing rights are written and enforced. This is genuine evidence that intra-MNE rights carry third-party teeth, though it documents the tax-legal construct of ownership more than de facto managerial control.
  • [inference] The binary ownership measure almost certainly understates the economic heterogeneity of rights: a subsidiary owning one minor trademark and one owning the firm's core platform technology both score 1, and graduated ownership, joint ownership, or rights over only part of an asset's life are invisible. If the theory is about the strength of residual claims, the treatment is measured with error that likely attenuates effects.
  • [inference] The channel decomposition is untested: control rights and income rights are bundled in the ownership variable and in the theory, so the DDD cannot say whether the response to R&D-opportunity shocks comes from faster approval, stronger motivation, or both. The paper's own Table 8 (no differential R&D spending) supports a project-selection story, but a design that varied income rights holding control constant (or vice versa) would be needed to isolate situated responsibility as the mechanism.
  • [open question] Whether the effects reflect genuine invention or patent assignment and filing behavior remains unresolved: because rights determine which entity legally owns what inventors create, an ownership change can move patent counts between subsidiaries without moving the underlying inventive activity. Distinguishing real from reallocated invention is the paper's deepest unresolved question, and the author's own "few changes in ownership" limitation means the data cannot yet answer it.

6. Conclusion

Magelssen establishes three things. First, the theoretical claim that intra-firm ownership rights are non-credible fails precisely where firms are organized as legally separate subsidiaries: transfer-pricing law makes internal rights allocations enforceable and costly to reverse, so property rights are a real governance instrument inside the MNE. Second, on evidence, the subsidiaries that hold those rights produce more patents, patents with more external citations, and — most tellingly — convert exogenous improvements in R&D opportunity into innovation while non-owners do not, with the gap concentrated in technologically advantaged locations where control-by-the-expert should matter. Third, there is an identifiable strategic cost to the tax-avoidance allocation of rights: when ownership sits in income-shielding locations, the R&D units best placed to innovate produce fewer and less impactful innovations. The one-line takeaway for strategy is that the placement of property rights over intangible assets is not a tax back-office detail but a first-order decision about who inside the firm will bear responsibility for, and therefore shape, its technological trajectory.

"An insight is that since ownership confers control rights, the subsidiaries that hold ownership rights can shape the technological trajectory of the firm."
— Magelssen (2020), Discussion (manuscript version)

Framing: the same logic that makes ownership a solution between firms — residual rights allocate both authority and accountability — turns out to operate inside firms wherever legal separation and third-party enforcement make rights credible.
